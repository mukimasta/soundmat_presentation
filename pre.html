USB-C PD trigger
20V
From any laptop brick or power bank
Why high then low
20V down the cable. 5V at the load.
45 W at 5V draws 9 A — wires get fat and voltage sags. At 20V it's 2.3 A; the buck steps down close to the loads. Same wall plug, half the copper.
Protect the data line
Star GND · 1000µF · 330Ω
WS2812B blinks burn through 6 A of inrush. Star-grounding stops shared returns from talking to the Pi; the bulk cap absorbs the spike at the strip; a 330Ω damper at the first LED kills DIN ringing.
Firmware

The whole ESP32 program, in four lines

10
Main loop
loop() { scan_sensors(); // drive ring HIGH, sweep two muxes, ADC × 256 send_sensor_data(); // S frame → Pi receive_led_data(); // L frame ← Pi (non-blocking) update_leds(); // WS2812B via RMT }
No threads. No acks. No retries. A scan finishes, the frame ships. The Pi answers when it can; the buffer is sipped at the top of each tick. A bad checksum just drops — environmental music never notices a missing frame.
ESP32 → Pi
S frame · 256 raw ADC reads
S:4095,4093,4090,2031,…,3498*A7\n
ASCII, comma-separated, XOR-checksum. Inner rings ship 32 values too — the Pi folds them down. Tail it in any serial monitor.
Pi → ESP32
L frame · 108 RGB triplets
L:000000,0A0500,1A0F00,…,000000*3E\n
Same envelope, hex payload. No L frame this tick? Repeat the last buffer. The light always has something to do.

Lessons

Three things that didn't work the first time

15
Hardware
PD Trigger v1 → v2
First PD trigger board refused to negotiate 20V no matter what we tried — locked at 5V. Replacement board worked first try. Lesson: read the PD chip datasheet before buying. Not all "PD Trigger" boards actually negotiate.
USB PD
Multimeter debug
Reliability
Flying wires → breakout board
First attempt: cut a Micro-USB cable, solder bare 28 AWG wires directly to the buck output. They snapped every time we moved the device. Replaced with a breakout board with screw terminals — sturdy, swappable, demo-ready.
28 AWG too thin
Mechanical fatigue
Design
The Lo-Fi paradox
"Sounds good no matter how you place it" fights "I can feel my placement affecting the music." We split the answer across two modes — and gave Jam its R0/R1 control stones as a deliberate skill-floor that pays off in tactile feedback.
