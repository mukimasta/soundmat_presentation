Electronic system

Power, compute, audio — under one shell

06
Power architecture
One USB-C cable. Outdoor capable.
PD trigger requests 20V from a phone charger or power bank — high voltage, low current means thin wires and minimal line loss. A buck converter steps it down to 5V close to the loads.
Compute split
Pi for sound. ESP32 for sensing.
Pi 3B has no ADC. ESP32 has 12-bit ADC, scans the 224-cell array at 20Hz, ships raw frames over UART.
Audio
"Pi's PWM audio is the ceiling on this installation's quality."
External USB DAC → 3.5mm → external speaker. Plug-and-play, no driver setup.
Photo
Hand-soldered perfboard
Top-down view. ESP32 socket, dual 16:1 MUX, level shifter, decoupling caps.

45W
All 108 LEDs at full brightness + Pi under load. Typical ~22W at 30% LED.
Reliability
Breadboard → perfboard
Public installations can't tolerate intermittent contact. Every connection now soldered through-hole; ICs in DIP sockets for easy swap.
Firmware

Scan, ship, light — every 50 ms

07
Scan loop
Row-scan with ghost-path suppression
For each of 8 rings, drive HIGH and ground the rest (kills ghost paths). Sweep the 32-slice column through dual 16:1 MUX. 256 ADC reads per frame.

Lessons

Three things that didn't work the first time

12
Hardware
PD Trigger v1 → v2
First PD trigger board refused to negotiate 20V no matter what we tried — locked at 5V. Replacement board worked first try. Lesson: read the PD chip datasheet before buying. Not all "PD Trigger" boards actually negotiate.
USB PD
Multimeter debug
Reliability
Flying wires → breakout board
First attempt: cut a Micro-USB cable, solder bare 28 AWG wires directly to the buck output. They snapped every time we moved the device. Replaced with a breakout board with screw terminals — sturdy, swappable, demo-ready.
28 AWG too thin
Mechanical fatigue
Design
The Lo-Fi paradox
"Sounds good no matter how you place it" fights "I can feel my placement affecting the music." We split the answer across two modes — and gave Jam its R0/R1 control stones as a deliberate skill-floor that pays off in tactile feedback.
