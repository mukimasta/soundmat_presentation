Power

The hardest part is feeding it

10
One cable, two rails, one ground
Peak load
45W
All 108 LEDs full white + Pi under load. Typically ~22 W at 30 % brightness.
Why 20 V on the wire
High voltage, thin cable, low loss
5 V at 9 A over a long run sags badly. Send 20 V, step down at the device — and any USB-C power bank runs it outdoors.
The real bug we designed out
LED current spikes browning out the Pi
WS2812B switching draws sharp transients. A 1000µF cap absorbs them; star grounding stops the Pi from riding the LED's voltage swings.
Firmware

One tight loop on the ESP32

11
The whole firmware
Scan → send → receive → light, forever
The one real subtlety
Ghost-path suppression
Drive one ring HIGH, force every other ring to GND — not floating. Floating rings leak current through pressed neighbours and invent presses that never happened.
for ring in 0..7:
  rings = GND; ring = HIGH
  read 32 slices via MUX
Robust because it's simple
Drop a bad frame. Never retransmit.
Both ends stream freely. Each frame carries an XOR checksum; if it fails, throw it away. In ambient music a single lost frame is imperceptible — and retries would only add latency.

Sonification · Jam mode

Place a stone in time — it's always in key

16
The scan line is a playhead
Angle is time. Ring is instrument.
R2–3 Bass R4–5 Melody R6–7 Lead
The core idea
You choose when.
It guarantees what.
"Always sounds good" and "feels like I did it" usually fight. We split them: where you place a stone controls rhythm and texture — harmony is never left to chance.
Harmony on rails
2m9 → 513 → 1maj9 → 69
An 8-bar chord loop written in key-relative degrees. Change key once and the whole piece — melody, bass, voicings — transposes together.
Lo-Fi, by hand
More stones, warmer sound
Total pressure on the center rings drives a master filter and tape drive — continuous, real-time. The room's mood knob.
Interaction

How you actually play it

17
Three moves, no manual
1
Drop a stone in the center
The loop wakes up and the scan line starts sweeping. The center is the play button.
2
Place stones around the rings
As the line sweeps past each stone, it plays a note — the outer ring sets the instrument, the angle sets the timing.
3
Add, move, take away — together
The loop keeps playing while strangers edit it live. Remove a stone and its part simply drops out.
No screen, no start menu
The first stone is the only instruction
Remove every stone and it falls quiet and resets. Nothing to read, nothing to press — you learn it by watching one other person.
